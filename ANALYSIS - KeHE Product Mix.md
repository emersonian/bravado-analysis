## What the reliable data (2020–2025) shows

**The assortment has narrowed sharply.** KeHE bought 13 distinct SKUs in 2020 and 11 in 2023. By 2025, only **six** SKUs had any KeHE revenue at all, and two of those were negligible ($678 and $3,862). The working set is effectively **four products**: Pineapple & Habanero, Ghost Pepper & Blueberry, Arbol Chili & Garlic, and Creamy Herb & Jalapeño.

**Revenue peaked in 2023 and has fallen 72% since.** $341,559 (2023) → $237,412 (2024) → $96,219 (2025).

**Two of the three historical anchors collapsed in 2025**, not gradually: Black Garlic & Carolina Reaper fell from $45,620 to $3,862 (−92%), and Garlic & Arbol Moruga Scorpion from $26,214 to $678 (−97%). Whether those are delistings, a supply failure, or a data artifact is not determinable from the ledger — but they are the kind of change a distributor communicates in writing, and no such correspondence is in the investor materials.

## Why this matters to the forecast

The July 2026 plan projects converting "KeHE placeholders into confirmed POs (Sep/Oct/Nov at ~$12K each)," and the August 24 briefing lists the same as a key risk: "velocity-based, not yet confirmed."

Against the record, $12,000 per month is roughly **50% above the 2025 monthly average** of $8,018 — from a relationship that has declined every year since 2023 and whose assortment has contracted from thirteen SKUs to four. The August briefing also reports that the one KeHE PO that did land in the window paid **$4,736.72 against a promised ~$9,573** — 49%.

**Questions this supports, all answerable from documents:** the current KeHE authorization list by SKU and region; any delisting or discontinuation notices received since 2024; the actual POs behind the Sep/Oct/Nov projections; and KeHE's velocity reports, which are the basis the plan itself cites.
