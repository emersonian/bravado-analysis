## 2. Answers by question

### Q1. The near deadline — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** Real, for negligent misrepresentation only. Two years (Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code 16.003(a)); Texas appeals courts start it when the statement induced the buyer to act (*Weaver & Tidwell*, Dallas 2014; *Wooten*, Dallas 2023). For the $35,000 that is signing on November 26, 2024, so the last day is Thanksgiving. The safe date is Wednesday, November 25.
- **Why it may not be worth saving.** The claim rests on a statement inside the contract, and a 2026 Houston decision requires an injury "distinct, separate, and independent" from the contract loss. Contract and fraud run to 2028; the Securities Act claim to 2027 or early 2028.
- **Tolling.** Enforceable for a named claim and a defined period (*Godoy v. Wells Fargo*, Tex. 2019). Nobody can be made to sign. The alternative is to file and serve.
- **Open.** Whether to preserve it at all, and whether the loan correction should go first.

### Q2. Correcting the loan — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** If the advance is a loan of money, $1,000 on $7,500 for a month exceeds either ceiling (10% default, Fin. Code 302.001(b); up to 18% under chapter 303 for parties "to a written agreement"). On a commercial loan a creditor who "contracts for or receives" excess interest owes three times the excess (305.001(a-1)), about $2,670–$2,815, plus the borrower's attorney's fees (305.005). Agreeing is enough; who proposed the term is irrelevant (*Leteff v. Roberts*, Houston 2018). Forfeiture of principal applies only to personal or household loans (305.002(b)).
- **Cure.** No liability if, within 60 days of actual discovery, the creditor corrects and gives written notice before the borrower does (305.103). Two later windows exist: 60 days after the borrower's own notice, and during a 60-day pause if it is raised as a counterclaim, when the lender must offer the borrower's fees (305.006).
- **Delivery.** "Personally, by telecopier, or by United States mail." Email and text are not listed. A 2026 decision rejected a correction that left one charge unaddressed (*Vu v. Medina*).
- **Open.** Which ceiling applies (about $150 of difference); whether a stablecoin is "money" (no Texas authority); the wording.

### Q3. Records demand — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** Send one for Comatri alone. Current text of Bus. Orgs. Code 101.502(a): a member "on written demand stating a proper purpose" may examine records "reasonably related to and appropriate to examine and copy for that proper purpose"; emails and texts are excluded; the demand goes to "a manager … at the limited liability company's principal office." Copies of the company agreement and tax returns are free on written request (101.502(c)).
- **Fees need a refusal.** 101.503 applies to a company that "refuses" a compliant demand. Courts have denied fees where the company was slow but eventually produced (*Pacific World Energy*, 2020). The November 19 request for a login is not that demand.
- **Other bases.** Operating Agreement 4.6 and Agreement 2 §1(a) have no purpose test. Contract fees under 38.001 need damages, so an access-only order earns none.
- **Scope.** The SBA loan file, payroll register, employer copies of W-2s and the journal-entry attachments are company records; ask for personal identifiers redacted.
- **Open.** Timing against any tolling request; whether a buyer's interest taints "proper purpose."

### Q4. Preservation — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** Send before the revised statements. A letter creates no claim; it fixes the date the company knew (*Brookshire Bros. v. Aldridge*, Tex. 2014). The client's own duty probably began in August 2026, when he first drafted a note to prospective counsel.
- **Keep:** Signal and text threads with disappearing messages off; wallet and exchange records; every version of the published pages; superseded memos.

### Q5. Material already shared — REPLACE
- **Answer.** Little of it is protected. Privilege needs a lawyer; voluntary disclosure waives; and Texas protects sharing between parties only through lawyers in a pending action (*In re XL Specialty*). Defamation has a one-year limit and a truth defence; a publisher may correct proactively.
- **What to restrict now** (after preserving copies):
  - The published financial page: "15 / 15 Discrepancies," three healthcare rows, "(personal card)" labels, and "repayable as $8,500."
  - The published page about the former CFO.
  - The two August briefs for the co-investor ("He knew," "ranked misrepresentations," healthcare providers named).
- **New fact for the letter.** The co-investor handed "the financial analysis" to the manager and former CFO on November 18.

### Q6. Is self-set pay owed? — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** A book entry is not a debt. He must show member approval after disclosure, or fairness. Bus. Orgs. Code 101.255(b) validates an interested transaction if disinterested governing persons approve, or "the members in good faith approve," or it "is fair to the company." *In re Estate of Poe* (Tex. 2022): "The burden was on the director (as fiduciary) to prove that the contract was fair." *Kott v. Miller* (Austin 2025, an LLC case): the burden is on the interested person, and an item appearing in the company's financial records as a liability did not establish disclosure.
- **His best argument.** An implied agreement at his earlier payroll rate for 2023–2024.
- **Open.** Whether his own 65% can supply approval; whether the January 2025 workbook counts as disclosure; which years are at risk.

### Q7. If the draws were compensation — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** The label does not bind the IRS ("The name by which the remuneration for services is designated is immaterial," Reg. 31.3401(a)-1). Netting is a wage payment on the netting date: an amount deducted from pay "is considered to be paid to the employee … at the time that the deduction is made" (Reg. 31.3123-1). Payroll tax on $176,914 is roughly $27,000 before penalties, and the responsible person can be personally liable (IRC 6672).
- **Three labels, three results.** Loan (what the books and all three returns say): the asset stays and he owes it. Compensation: payroll returns for 2023–2026 to correct, his income, nothing owed back. Distribution: no deduction, and the members' decision was required.
- **A tension for him.** Pay left unpaid beyond 2½ months is deferred compensation with a 20% penalty unless paying would have threatened the company as a going concern (Reg. 1.409A-1(b)(4)). Avoiding that requires saying he had no binding right until now, which undercuts "owed since 2023."
- **Open.** Which treatment Comatri should insist on. Loan preserves the company's claim.

### Q8. The 2026 distributions — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** Probably not valid as distributions. Operating Agreement 3.2: "The Members shall determine and distribute available funds." The statutory default is "an equal vote" per member and a majority of members (101.354, 101.355). No determination is recorded.
- **Recovery.** 101.206 bars a distribution when liabilities exceed the fair value of assets; a member must return it only if he "had knowledge of the violation"; "reasonable compensation for present or past services" is excluded; suit must start within two years of the distribution.
- **Counter.** The label is software automation and the payments were pay, which folds this into Q6 and Q7.
- **Figure to fix.** The comparable amount is $18,502. The account shows $21,752 because it includes a $3,250 check to a third party.

### Q9. Suing for the company — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** Yes, alone and without demand: fewer than 35 members (101.463). A court may treat it as direct "if justice requires," but recovery "may be paid directly to the plaintiff or to the limited liability company if necessary to protect the interests of creditors," so expect it to go to the company. The plaintiff must be a member when suing, so retitling the interest could break standing.
- **Practical value.** An injunction and stopping the balance being erased.
- **Company-paid defence.** Operating Agreement 4.8 excludes actions "by or in the right of the Company." In *Mundy* the manager was ordered to repay $107,417 of defence costs.

### Q10. Comatri's 2024 claims — SPLIT INTO THREE
- **Answer.** A claim exists and reaches the manager personally, but it is a settlement lever more than a case to run to judgment. Rated moderate on the $15,010 and weak to moderate on the $35,000.
- **Law.** Gov't Code 4008.052: liable for a sale "by means of an untrue statement of a material fact or an omission"; the seller must prove the buyer knew; an issuer has no reasonable-care defence for "a writing prepared and delivered by the issuer in the sale." Control persons are jointly liable (4008.055). Remedy is rescission with interest (4008.056); fees if equitable (4008.060). A buyer "has no duty of due diligence," and individuals who solicited were "sellers" (*Davis v. MSR Holding*, Houston 2024, on LLC units).
- **Time.** Three years from discovery, five from sale; registration claims three years from sale (4008.062).
- **Trap.** After a qualifying rescission offer the buyer must reject "in writing within 30 days" and "expressly reserve" the right to sue, then sue within a year.
- **Weak points.** The October 2, 2024 email disclosed a negative balance, a $100,000 funding need, factoring and a pending SBA loan; the workbook saved October 17, 2024 shows a debt schedule; 22 months of silence; "Payment has already been collected" in Agreement 1; rescission means handing back the interest.

### Q11. Who is the borrower — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** Arguably both. An agent who contracts for an undisclosed principal is personally liable (*Latch v. Gratty*); the company later listed the $7,500 as its own debt. The SBA statement adds little: the same day he wrote "They won't approve my next 300k until i catch up on payments."

### Q12. Tax date and what not to sign — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** Termination is "effective on and after the date of cessation" (IRC 1362(d)(2)(B)): July 22, 2024 on the agreements. Comatri is owed no K-1 on any correct reading. The open question is whether a 2024 K-1 was filed for its shares under another name.
- **Relief is unlikely.** It needs a private ruling (fee up to $43,700), every shareholder's agreement, and can treat the ineligible shareholder as an S shareholder for the period. The shortcut procedure does not cover this.
- **Do not sign without tax advice:** any retitling or nominee statement (a transfer to the owner also forfeits Comatri's loss, IRC 267, 311); any acquisition date other than July 22 and November 26, 2024; any IRS-facing consent; a W-9 in the client's own name; any acknowledgment that Comatri caused the termination; any approval of the netting; any bank or payroll signing authority.
- **Useful right.** A corporation's return is open to "any bona fide shareholder of record owning 1 percent or more" (IRC 6103(e)(1)(D)(iii)).

### Q13. Changing the manager — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** Not without his vote. A receiver is available on the insolvency ground alone (11.404(a)(1)(A)) but only if "all other available legal and equitable remedies … are inadequate," and the applicant in practice funds it. There is no oppression buyout (*Ritchie v. Rupe*).
- **Realistic tool.** A temporary injunction in a suit for the company, limiting pay and personal expenses (*Mundy v. Savell*, 2026).

### Q14. Buying the assets — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** The client cannot force a sale, and the manager alone can sell, to anyone (Operating Agreement 4.3(a)). No route gives clean title without the SBA: its lien follows the assets unless it consents. A buyer is not liable for unassumed debts (Bus. Orgs. Code 10.254(b)). An involuntary bankruptcy needs three creditors with undisputed claims, or one if there are fewer than twelve.
- **Open.** Liens, guarantees, and who owns the trademark and recipes.

### Q15. The SBA application — REWORD AND MOVE
- **Answer.** No evidence means no duty to report. The risk is linkage: federal law punishes whoever "under a threat of informing, or as a consideration for not informing, against any violation of any law of the United States, demands or receives any money or other valuable thing" (18 U.S.C. 873). Texas treats threats "to accuse a person of any offense" as coercion.
- **Rules.** Never mention the SBA, reporting or "federal" in any demand, message or meeting. Request the loan file as a company record for member purposes. Take advice on any report separately. Do not share this question with the co-investors.
- **Context.** Economic-injury loan proceeds may not be used for "disbursements to owners … except for reasonable remuneration" (13 CFR 123.303(b)). Whether this loan is of that type is undocumented.

### Q16. One lawyer for everyone — SHARPEN
- **Answer.** Probably Comatri and the client personally only. The Nguyens' interests diverge on the tax date, as lenders, and as possible sellers' creditors. A "common-interest" arrangement does not protect sharing before a suit is pending.

### Q17. Sequence and cost — SHARPEN
