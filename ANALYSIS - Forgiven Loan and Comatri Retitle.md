## 1. Every written statement that the $69,440 Tallerine line of credit is "forgiven"

| Date | Document | Exact language |
|---|---|---|
| **Jul 21, 2026** | Eight-page "State of the Business" (shared privately with Blake Benthall the day before the meeting) | Debt table row: *"Tallerine line of credit — $69,440 — **Forgiven** — My father's contribution. **He has forgiven it; I keep it on the books because I intend to repay him one day**"* |

| **Aug 24, 2026** | Investor briefing PDF | **No mention at all.** The words "Tallerine," "forgiven," and the figure $69,440 do not appear anywhere. Total debt is stated as **$572,715** "excluding trade A/P, past-due rent, and the disputed freight claim (all shown separately)" — the $69,440 is silently absent from both the total and the itemization. |

Points that follow directly from the records:

1. **The two July statements contradict each other and themselves.** The private version says simultaneously that the loan is forgiven *and* that he intends to repay it — a loan one intends to repay is not forgiven. The circulated version deleted that sentence and kept only "Forgiven."
2. **No forgiveness has ever been recorded.** The Anthony Tallerine LOC sits at its full $69,440 in QuickBooks (account 33100) and in the Finaloop balance sheet through the July 23, 2026 export. Neither cash-plan debt schedule (July 22 or August 24 workbook) lists it or any forgiveness of it. (Debt forgiveness would also be cancellation-of-indebtedness income flowing through the members' K-1s — nothing of the kind appears.)
3. **The same lender was re-borrowed from six days after the meeting.** On July 28, 2026 the father wired a **new $10,000 loan** (from Veronica M. Tallerine), which the August cash plan schedules for full repayment inside 2026 — $2,500 on Oct 9, Nov 12, Dec 9, and Dec 21, the last annotated *"$10K fully repaid IN 2026 (opens door to re-borrow)."* The family lender is treated as active and priority-repaid while investors were told the earlier balance was forgiven.

## 2. When Comatri's investment became "Blake Benthall" equity

**In QuickBooks (per the audit log), the money was never Blake's or Comatri's by name:**

| Date | User | Event |
|---|---|---|
| Apr 22, 2024 | William S. Annon, CPA | Creates named member-equity accounts — **Clark, Nguyen, Tallerine, Nelson**. No account is ever created for Comatri or Benthall. |
| Jul 23, 2024 | Strata Cloud Accountants | Adds customer "Coinbase.com"; completes the $15,010 deposit (received Jul 22). |
| Aug 8–16, 2024 | Strata Cloud Accountants | Edits, unmatches, deletes and re-books the $15,010; on **Aug 16, 2024** creates account **"Member Equity - Coinbase"** and posts it there. |
| Dec 2024 | Strata Cloud Accountants | Books the $35,000 (received Dec 2) to the same account. |

There is **no rename, edit, or reclassification of that account by Jeremiah — or anyone — anywhere in the QuickBooks audit log** through August 24, 2026. In QuickBooks the account is still "Member Equity - Coinbase" today. The audit log therefore shows the retitle did **not** happen in QuickBooks.

**It happened in the Finaloop migration.** The Finaloop ledger (exported July 23, 2026) contains an account named **"Capital - Blake Benthall"** holding:

- **$50,010** — entry titled *"2026 opening balance adjustment,"* source *"Finaloop equity adjustment,"* system-dated **Dec 31, 2025**. This is exactly Comatri's $15,010 + $35,000.
- **$4,600** — a "CAPITAL ONE - TRANSFER" into the Found account on **May 1, 2026**, booked directly to Blake's personal capital (Blake is verifying whether Comatri sent this at all).

Timing bounds: Finaloop's first subscription charge is **January 7, 2026** ($245/month thereafter), so the migration and its opening balances were configured between early January 2026 and the July 23, 2026 export; the opening entry itself is back-dated to Dec 31, 2025 as migration entries are. Finaloop provides no user-level audit log in the records, so the person who keyed the mapping cannot be identified from these files — but it is the founder's system (the July documents state he files corrections with Finaloop directly), and **no consent, member resolution, or communication in the records authorizes treating Comatri, Inc.'s investment as Blake Benthall's personal capital.** Comatri's name appears nowhere in either accounting system.

**Why it matters:** an S corporation cannot have a corporate shareholder. Retitling Comatri's equity to Blake personally in the books is precisely the change Jer pressured Blake to consent to — and the new ledger reflects it as already done, before and without that consent. This is a concrete, dated item for the records request: the December 2025 Finaloop report, the migration workpapers, any Form 2553 and shareholder consents, and the K-1s will show whose name the company reports to the IRS.
