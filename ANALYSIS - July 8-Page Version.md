## 4. Genuinely new information in this version

1. **K-1s and the 2024 tax return exist and are quantified**: "our K-1 filings are cash basis, so the 2024 tax return shows a smaller loss (**$67,869**) than the accrual books"; appendix: 2024 federal return shows **$862,582 gross receipts, $67,869 ordinary loss**, cash basis.

   **What this does and does not prove.** K-1s are issued only by *pass-through* entities — partnerships (Form 1065) and S corporations (Form 1120-S). A C corporation issues none (it files Form 1120 and reports dividends on 1099-DIV). So this confirms Bravado filed as a pass-through for 2024, but it does **not** by itself distinguish partnership from S corp. **The decisive test is the form number printed at the top of the K-1:**
   - **"Schedule K-1 (Form 1065)"** = taxed as a **partnership**. A C corporation such as Comatri is a **perfectly legal partner** — meaning there was no tax obstacle to Comatri's ownership in 2024, and any later S election is what would create the conflict (and would have been made knowing Comatri was a member).
   - **"Schedule K-1 (Form 1120-S)"** = an **S election was already in effect**. Corporations are ineligible S shareholders (IRC §1361), so Comatri's Nov 2024 admission would have invalidated or terminated the election — unless the return simply did not report Comatri as an owner, which is its own problem.

   Two separate factual questions therefore matter: **(a) which form number** appears on the 2024/2025 K-1s, and **(b) who received them — was Comatri, Inc. on the list?** (Neither Blake nor, to be confirmed, the Nguyens recall receiving one.) Note also the motive dimension: an S election typically benefits the founder personally (self-employment-tax savings on his own earnings), while the cure being demanded — moving Comatri's interest into Blake's personal name — transfers the cost and the personal tax exposure to Blake.
2. **Shopify Capital ($8,626, "16% of Shopify sales, due June 2027") was disclosed here** — then dropped from the v4 table and from the August briefing's debt schedule. (This corrects the earlier finding that it was "never disclosed": disclosed privately once, then omitted.)
3. **A/P math ties**: "$150,009 after removing $20,231 of old bills already paid (correction filed with Finaloop)" — QuickBooks A/P at 12/31/2025 was $170,240; 170,240 − 20,231 = 150,009 exactly. A fair claim.
4. **"Payroll tax catch-up $0–$3K"** — admits June–July 2026 team pay ran outside payroll with W-2/1099 exposure "capped at the June–July window (~$13K gross)."
5. **Partner narrative expanded**: "he signed over his equity and left" — with no mention that the books carry a **$56,369 receivable from James Nelson**, nor that the exit was booked July 2023.
6. The margin basis note is more careful here (Finaloop Jan 1–Jul 19; "compare the trend within each basis, not across them") — a caveat that got weaker in v4's table presentation.
