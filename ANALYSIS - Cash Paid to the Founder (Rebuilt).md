# Cash Paid to the Founder — Rebuilt from the Ledgers

**Prepared:** September 1, 2026. **Supersedes** the founder-payment figures in `ANALYSIS - Reimbursement Legitimacy.md` §2, `BRIEF - Meeting with Hugh Nguyen.md`, and Figures B, C, D and F of the published analysis, all of which have been corrected to match this memo.

**Question raised (Mark Clark, Aug 2026):** *most of the payments shown to the founder were probably his accrued wages.*

**Answer in one paragraph.** The company's own books say otherwise, in both ledgers and in every year. From January 2023 through July 22, 2026 the company paid $335,075 to Jeremiah Tallerine or his personal creditors, he returned $14,362, and $89,572 of the net was matched by itemized reimbursement entries. Of the remaining $231,141, the bookkeepers charged **$39,992** against his accrued salary — all of it in 2024, plus a $2,000 plug in 2025 — and classified everything else as a reimbursement advance, a draw, a distribution, or an amount he owes back. The accrued-wage liability of **$176,914** carried in both QuickBooks and Finaloop is, by construction, the salary that was *not* paid. If the payments were instead treated as wages, they exceed the entire 2023–2025 salary accrual ($212,558 gross) by roughly $18,600, the $176,914 liability would not exist, and the founder would still owe the company money. Either the payments were not wages (the books' position) or there are no accrued wages to convert. Both cannot be true.

## 1. Method

**Sources.** QuickBooks general ledger, January 1, 2020 – March 27, 2026 (116,499 lines, 192 accounts; the official year-end profit-and-loss and balance-sheet exports reproduce from it to the dollar). Finaloop general ledger, January 1 – July 23, 2026 (81,710 lines). Mark Clark's "Jer Accts" workbook tab (January 16, 2025). Every figure below was recomputed from those files; nothing is carried forward from earlier memos.

**What counts as cash to the founder.** A line in a company bank or company credit-card account whose offsetting account is one of the founder's four QuickBooks accounts — *33500 Due to Jeremiah*, *Due from Jer*, *53000 Jer's Draw*, *Wages Due to Jeremiah* — plus:
- payments of his personal cards and personal-account transfers that the bookkeepers charged straight to an expense account instead of a founder account (2020–2025: $12,141 in total, listed in §6);
- Expensify-era reimbursement bills paid to the vendor "Jeremiah Tallerine" (2020–2022: $24,959, itemized);
- the Gusto "Owner's Draw" pay runs of 2020 ($36,268) and the one Gusto paycheck of September 18, 2024 funded through his personal account ($2,347);
- for 2026, Finaloop's *Distributions – Jeremiah Tallerine*, *Reimbursements to owners*, personal transactions charged to his owner account, three Citi card payments Finaloop left as "unidentified," a $250 check for a New York lease application, and three July transfers to his Capital One account not yet classified.

**Excluded, and why.** Check 2418 of September 10, 2020 for $14,078 (booked to his draw, reclassified by the CPA on January 1, 2021 as James Nelson's 2019 federal income tax); every charge on "Credit card (9532)" — that card autopays under the name *Nelson, James M* and its 2022 charges were payments to Endorphin Farms, not to the founder; journal entries, year-end sweeps, and other non-cash reclassifications (these are used to classify the cash, not counted as cash); the 2021–2022 W-2 salary paid through Gusto, which the aggregate payroll entries do not separate by employee (his semi-monthly gross was $3,125.01 — $75,000 a year — per the 2023 "skipped paycheck" entries).

**Classification.** Each cash line carries (a) the *channel* — transfer to his personal bank, payment of a personal card or loan, personal spending on a company card, Gusto — and (b) the *book treatment*, which is simply the account the bookkeepers charged. "Itemized" means the bookkeepers posted a journal entry crediting his account with line-by-line business-expense categories, or paid a vendor invoice he had covered personally. The itemized amount for each year is the sum of those credits (54 entries; every one was checked for expense-account splits).

**Corrections to earlier figures.** (1) 2022 was overstated at $46,200 because the Endorphin Farms charges on James Nelson's card were counted as cash to the founder; the cash to him was $15,000 plus draws. (2) 2023 was understated at $30,300 because the draw account was omitted; $43,292 of transfers booked to *Jer's Draw* — mostly $2,300 twice a month — were cash. (3) 2024 "documented" was overstated at $45,047 because it included the CPA's $18,069 year-end sweep, which is the opposite of documentation. (4) 2026 adds $2,106 of items Finaloop classified outside the owner accounts. The monthly chart already used the fuller 2023 figures; the annual table did not, so the two disagreed. They now agree.

Three things follow:

1. **Only $39,992 was ever charged against his salary — 12% of the net cash since 2023.** Every one of those 29 transfers is a 2024 transfer to his personal Chase account, and the bookkeepers' memos on them read "owners draw." The 2025 attempt to charge $23,416 to wages was reversed by the accountants themselves within weeks.
2. **The $176,914 accrued-wage liability assumes none of the other $280,000 was wages.** It is the arithmetic residue of $212,558 accrued (2023: $68,750; 2024: $74,775; 2025: $69,033) less $37,992 paid, plus the $2,000 tie-out plug. Finaloop carried it forward unchanged ("Wages Due to Jeremiah, OB (based on BS)") and shows no 2026 paydown.
3. **The same ledgers say he owes the company.** Finaloop's July 2026 balance sheet carries *Due to/from owner – Jeremiah Tallerine* at **$226,744** (an asset: money he owes) alongside *Payroll payable* of **$176,914**. Net: **$49,830 owed by him**, before questioning any accrual. That is the founder's own current bookkeeping, not ours.

## 4. The "probably wages" hypothesis, tested

Suppose every undocumented dollar since January 2023 were re-labelled salary:

| | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Net cash to founder, Jan 2023 – Jul 22, 2026 | $320,713 |
| less: matched by itemized reimbursement entries | (89,572) |
| **Cash with no expense documentation** | **$231,141** |
| Gross salary accrued 2023–2025 (the whole basis of the $176,914) | $212,558 |
| **Excess of undocumented cash over the entire three-year accrual** | **$18,583** |

So under Mark's reading the wages are not merely paid; they are overpaid, the $176,914 disappears from the balance sheet (and from any debt-to-equity conversion), and the founder is still in the company's debt by roughly $18,600 before the 2026 payments are considered at all. The accrual is also a *gross* figure with no payroll-tax component (AJE-547 has none), while the cash went out untaxed; treating the cash as wages would create an unremitted-withholding problem on top.

Under the books' reading, which the outside accountants applied consistently for four years — reimbursement when itemized, receivable when not, wages only when someone said so — the founder was paid $89,572 of documented reimbursements and $39,992 of salary, and took $191,149 more (net of what he returned) that nobody documented — which is why Finaloop shows him owing $226,744 while being owed $176,914.

Either way the conclusion for the members is the same: **there is no version of the books in which both the payments and the accrual stand.**

## 6. What "itemized" does and does not mean

- **Itemized (2022–2025: $107,364).** Journal entries crediting his account with named expense categories — monthly "Jer Reimbursement" summaries in 2023 (up to 13 lines each), "Electricity reimbursement summary" and "Reimbursements – Kelly's collision" in 2024, and 23 smaller entries in 2025 ("Reimbursement to Jer – Roll labels" $49.48; "– coverdash" $487.08; "Hertz, Shell Oil, Walmart" $4,820.93). Also vendor invoices he paid personally: Endorphin Farms $15,000 net (Dec 2022), Erewhon slotting $6,077 (Dec 2023), Specialty Bottling $1,500 and $1,872 (2024), EZCOM $1,019 (2024). "Itemized" means the bookkeepers received line detail; it does not mean receipts were audited. Two 2025 entries are generic ("Jer's reimbursement 05.08.2025" $6,412.70; the Hertz/Shell/Walmart entry, which post-dates his move).
- **Expensed directly without itemization ($8,101 in 2023–2025).** Payments of his personal Chase card 3360 booked straight to Travel, Office, Events and Shipping in mid-2023 ($5,479), a $2,000 transfer to his account booked to Website/Design (Aug 9, 2023), 2024 Citi autopays booked to credit-card fees ($356), and two 2025 Mastercard payments booked to bank fees ($103). A card bill booked to "Travel" is a classification, not documentation.
- **Not itemized ($199,038 gross since 2023, plus the 2022 sweep).** This is the accountants' own number, not ours: the year-end balances they moved to *Due from Jer* or left as a debit in 33500, plus his draw account, plus everything Finaloop has booked in 2026. The largest single components: 2025's $47,862 of transfers to his Capital One personal checking 5820 (21 transfers, 12 of them exactly $2,500; one memo'd "OWNERS BI-MONTHLY DRAW"), $15,596 to Chase personal 0002, $6,390 to "Get Your Virtual Card …3135 – Is it personal bank?", and $27,997 of personal card bills paid in January 2025 (Apple Card $10,891; Bank of America $10,104 and $1,713; Mastercard $1,465; Citi $1,347) in the four weeks after the $226,200 SBA disbursement of January 3, 2025.
