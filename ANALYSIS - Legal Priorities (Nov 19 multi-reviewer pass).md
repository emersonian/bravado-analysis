## 2. Priorities, ranked

### 1. The pending netting, 2026 salary and write-offs
- **Facts.** Mark Clark's Nov 19 email says the manager's balance and accrued wages "should be netted," that 2026 compensation "will need to be added," and that the former partner's $56,369 "likely needs to be written off." At the earlier $75,000 rate (an assumption; no rate is stated) the result is about $25,200 owed to the manager. No agreement, rate-setting or member approval is in the records. The 8-page July version sent privately to the client states the plan in the manager's words: "My back pay ... absorbs the $75K I have drawn; the ~$101K net I will likely forgive or collect over years."
- **Frameworks.** Self-interested transaction by a fiduciary: presumed unfair, burden on the fiduciary (*Holloway*; *Greathouse*); safe harbour for disclosed, disinterested approval or fairness (TBOC 101.255). Operating Agreement 5.1 sets a standard, no rate or approver; 4.7 shields only good-faith acts and only liability "to the Members." For creditors: an obligation incurred, and an insider's old claim satisfied, while liabilities exceed assets. For tax: netting is a wage payment, with roughly $66,000–$81,000 of deposits due against $1,963 of cash (illustrative).
- **Who holds it.** The company (a member may sue derivatively without demand in a closely held LLC, TBOC 101.463); creditors separately.
- **Strength.** Strong. It is in writing before it is done.
- **Best answer to it.** 5.1 entitles him to reasonable pay; the rate matches his earlier payroll rate; 4.3(f) lets managers compromise claims; netting is ordinary bookkeeping.
- **Gap.** The client's Nov 19 reply-all asked questions but stated no objection. Silence invites ratification and account-stated arguments.

### 2. Comatri's tax position and what it must not sign
- **Facts.** A statement attached to the 2025 Form 1120, signed by the manager on 10/10/2026 with its own penalties-of-perjury declaration, dates Comatri's acquisition January 1, 2025. Both agreements, both deposits, the 2024 return's paid-in capital and share count, and the 2024 K-1 worksheet place it in 2024.
- **Frameworks.** Termination takes effect on the date eligibility ends and is not elective (IRC 1362(d)(2)). A 2024 date splits 2024 into an S and a C short year and moves about 44% of each individual member's 2024 K-1 items back to the company. Relief for an inadvertent termination (1362(f)) needs Comatri's signature and its exit as holder.
- **Exposure.** Comatri appears to owe no tax of its own on any reading. Its exposure is what it signs. The statement also helps Comatri: it tells the IRS that Comatri Inc., not an individual, holds 1,000 shares.
- **New point.** The Operating Agreement liquidates by capital account while members paid different amounts per share, which may be a second class of stock. If so the election had a defect unrelated to Comatri. A tax practitioner must confirm.
- **Do not sign without independent tax advice:** anything changing the holder or date; any IRS-facing shareholder consent or statement; a W-9 for the interest in the client's own name; any acknowledgment that Comatri caused the termination; any release bundled with a "fix."
- **Conflict to note.** The Nguyens hold 2024 K-1s that a 2024 termination date would disturb. Their interest on the date differs from Comatri's.

### 3. Records access and preservation
- **Three bases for access:** Operating Agreement 4.6 (all books and records, no purpose test, at the member's expense); Agreement 2 §1(a) (contractual, and it ends if the manager stops being majority holder); TBOC 101.502 with fee-shifting on refusal (101.503).
- **Status.** No formal written demand has been made or refused, so no fee claim exists yet. The QuickBooks login granted Jan 27, 2025 still worked on Aug 24, 2026.
- **Route.** One counsel-signed demand that meets the statute strictly, states member purposes (not lender purposes), separates records from questions, and is sent before any other claim is filed.
- **Preservation.** Ask for the before-state and a list of every change, not a freeze. The duty binds the client too from Nov 19, 2026: Signal and text threads, wallet records, pre-July 2024 messages.

### 5. Comatri's own claims from 2024
- **Texas Securities Act** (Gov't Code 4008.052, 4008.055): the only theory that needs no proof of reliance, offers rescission (about $56,000 with interest), and reaches the manager personally as control person. Moderate. Whether an LLC interest is a "security" turns on the investment-contract test.
- **Contract representation §2(d)** ("no outstanding assessments or other liabilities"; ledger liabilities about $655,000 and $665,000 on the signing dates as the books now stand): strong on the broad reading, weak on the tax-only reading, and against the company only.
- **Weaknesses the other side will use.**
  - The tax-only reading is probably the stronger textual one; under it the evidence is a $6,441 deferred payroll-tax balance.
  - The Oct 2, 2024 email disclosed negative cash, a $100,000 funding need and a pending SBA loan; its sheets label Actual and Budget columns.
  - The client had a balance sheet, the manager's account line by line, and a QuickBooks login from Jan 27, 2025 (audit log), and raised nothing for about 22 months.
  - A workbook saved Oct 17, 2024 shows "Accrued salaries-Jer"; if it was delivered before Nov 26, 2024 the second-investment claim weakens further.
  - Both deposits reached the company from a Coinbase account in the manager's name; nothing in the file shows the transfer from Comatri.
  - Agreement 1 says "Payment has already been collected."
  - Rescission would end Comatri's standing as a member.
- **Double-edged:** no written member consent to Comatri's admission (Operating Agreement 1.9) is on file.

### 6. The client's April 2026 advance (personal exposure)
- **Facts.** 7,500 USDC for "a 8500 return in a month," requested by text in the first person, to a wallet the manager supplied. The company's own debt schedule lists "Blake Benthall $7,500."
- **Framework.** About 160% a year against an 18% commercial ceiling. A lender who "contracts for or receives" excess interest on a commercial transaction is liable for three times the excess, about $2,670, plus the borrower's fees (Fin. Code 305.001(a-1), 305.005). No principal forfeiture or criminal provision on the commercial reading.
- **Cure.** A written correction within 60 days of actual discovery, before the borrower gives notice (305.103).
- **Until then.** No letter, request or pleading should state $8,500 as owed. The borrower's identity is open; a notice would sensibly go to both the company and the manager.

### 7. Control and remedies: what is and is not available
- **Not available:** removal (needs a majority he holds); a common-law oppression buyout (*Ritchie v. Rupe*). A receiver is a last resort and the company has no cash to fund one. The manager can vote dissolution alone (Operating Agreement 1.4(a)).
- **Available to a 9% member alone:** records demand; preservation notice and written objections; refusing consents (amendment, new interests, new members, the tax fix); a derivative or declaratory claim.
- **Negotiated:** standstill; a note for the validated balance secured by his interest; pay fixed in advance by disinterested members; a second signer and monthly exports. A debt-for-interest redemption looks barred while liabilities exceed assets.
- **Joint representation with the Nguyens** has real conflicts: lender against equity, conversion price, the tax date, and no confidentiality between joint clients.

## 4. Risks to the client

1. **Usury** on the April advance (priority 6).
2. **Published and circulated material.** Two analysis pages are published by link, the corrections log has about eighteen entries, and earlier briefs for a co-investor used conclusory wording. Truth and common-interest protections do not cover wrong figures or outside readers.
3. **Privilege.** Memos written before counsel was engaged may not be protected. Sharing analysis outside the lawyer-client relationship can waive what protection exists.
4. **Silence as acceptance** on the netting and the 2026 salary.
5. **The S-corporation problem being attributed to Comatri,** and the admission-consent gap being turned on Comatri's standing.
6. **Cost.** The Operating Agreement may let the company fund the manager's defence, and a derivative recovery in a company with these liabilities benefits creditors first.
