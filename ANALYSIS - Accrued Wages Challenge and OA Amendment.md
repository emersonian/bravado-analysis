## Part 1 — "The plan does not depend on new borrowing," and the loan six days later

The statement appears **once in each July document**, in the section headed "The balance sheet, honestly," immediately after the sentence "Nothing is hidden":

- **July 21, 2026 — eight-page version, shared privately with Blake Benthall:**
  > "Second, **the plan does not depend on new borrowing.** It pays $107K of debt service out of H2 operating cash while still ending the year near $29K in the bank."

- **July 22, 2026 — v4, circulated to all investors:**
  > "**The plan does not depend on new borrowing:** it services $107K of debt out of H2 operating cash while still ending the year near $30.7K in the bank."

**Six days later — July 28, 2026 — a $10,000 loan was taken from the founder's father.** The company's own cash plan records it: *"IN: Anthony (Dad) loan $10K wire from Veronica M Tallerine · funds landed $10,000."*

**In fairness, three things must be said alongside that:**

1. **He disclosed it at the next update.** The August 24 briefing reports it in three separate places, including a line item ("Cash receipts – new loan (Anthony) … +$10,000") and a heading reading **"NEW BORROWING."** This was not concealed.
2. **Borrowing was within his authority.** Operating Agreement §4.3(d) expressly authorizes the Managers to borrow money. He did not need member approval.
3. **The stated reason is a real business purpose:** "raised to facilitate the production migration from SBG to John Henry's."

**What remains fairly criticizable:**

- The forecast investors were asked to rely on was contradicted within six days. Whether the need was foreseeable on July 22 is a fair question — **neither July document mentions John Henry's at all**, though John Henry's was an existing vendor with purchase orders in the 2025 ledger.
- The August briefing subtly narrows the original claim: it concludes "**No institutional borrowing added.**" The July statement said "new borrowing," without qualification. The qualifier appears only after the fact.
- **The lender is the same person whose earlier $69,440 line of credit both July documents described as "Forgiven."** A lender who has forgiven a debt does not usually lend again six days later — and this new loan is scheduled for **priority repayment ahead of trade creditors** ($2,500 on Oct 9, Nov 12, Dec 9 and Dec 21), with the plan's own annotation: *"$10K fully repaid IN 2026 (opens door to re-borrow)."*

## Part 2 — Grounds on which the $176,913.70 accrued-wage claim could be challenged

### 2.1 The Operating Agreement sets a standard, not a blank check

**Article 5.1 (Management Fee):** *"Any Manager rendering services to the Company shall be entitled to compensation **commensurate with the value of such services**."*

That is the entire authority for founder compensation. It creates an entitlement measured by an **objective standard** — the value of services rendered — and says nothing authorizing a Manager to set his own rate, to accrue unpaid amounts as a company liability, or to determine the amount without member involvement. Points that go to whether the accrued amounts meet the standard:

- The rates accrued ($68,750 in 2023, $74,775 in 2024, $69,033 in 2025) were charged while revenue fell from $1.75M to $465K and the company lost $564K, $295K and $272K in those same years.
- Per Blake's contemporaneous notes, the founder held outside employment during part of this period and relocated away from the company's Houston operations in early 2026.
- **No authorizing document exists in any record** — no employment agreement, no member resolution, no manager compensation approval. Item 26 of the records request asks for the "authorization or basis" for each category; if the answer is that none exists, the amount rests on §5.1's standard alone and is open to challenge on its merits.

### 2.2 The accruals were created retroactively, by the accountants, after year-end

The 2025 tranche — **AJE-547, $69,033, dated 12/31/2025 — was created on February 26, 2026**, by Strata Cloud Accountants, and subsequently edited three times by Mark Clark (March 31 and April 8, 2026). A liability recorded eight weeks after the period it covers, by third parties, is not evidence of a contemporaneous agreement to pay.

### 2.3 The entry has no payroll-tax component — so it may not be "wages" at all

AJE-547 consists of exactly two lines: debit **73902 Salaries & Wages $69,033**, credit **Wages Due to Jeremiah $69,033**. There is no employer payroll-tax accrual. Finaloop's balance sheet carries **"Payroll tax liability: $0"** against **"Payroll payable: $176,913.70."**

If $176,914 were genuinely accrued W-2 wages, the employer's share of FICA alone (7.65%) would be roughly **$13,500**, plus FUTA/SUTA — none of it booked, and none of it run through Gusto. Either the company's liabilities are understated by that amount, or the balance is not properly characterized as wages. Both readings help a challenge; the second suggests the item is better understood as an unsecured related-party claim.

### 2.4 It is subject to a larger offset that the company's own books already record

The same balance sheet shows **$226,743.59 due *from* the founder** (owner receivable — draws, distributions, personal expenses paid by the company). Net of the wage claim, **he owes the company $49,829.89.** Any assertion of the $176,914 has to contend with the receivable sitting opposite it.

### 2.5 The presentation of it to investors is itself a ground

The July 2026 documents told investors he had "skipped my own paychecks" while the books simultaneously recorded those paychecks as a debt owed to him — and the 2025 figures presented to investors **excluded AJE-547's effect entirely**, while the 2024 comparison column included that year's equivalent accrual. Whatever the merits of the underlying claim, the disclosure of it was not complete.

### 2.6 Solvency

Accruing and prioritizing officer compensation while the company is balance-sheet insolvent and trade creditors go past due raises questions under Texas law that counsel should evaluate directly (including TBOC restrictions on distributions to members when a company is insolvent, and whether any portion of this is properly characterized as a distribution rather than compensation).

## Part 3 — Do members have to consent before wages are converted to equity?

**Two independent routes. The second is much stronger.**

### Route 1 — §1.9 (weak on its literal terms)

> **1.9 ADMISSION OF ADDITIONAL MEMBERS.** "…no additional **members** may be admitted to the Company through issuance by the company of a new interest in the Company, without the prior unanimous written consent of the Members."

This restricts **admitting additional members**. Issuing additional shares to Jeremiah Tallerine — already a member — arguably does not "admit an additional member," so §1.9 may not be triggered on its face. **It plainly *is* triggered if Anthony Tallerine's line of credit is converted**, because that would admit a person who is not currently a member: Blake or Hugh could each individually block that.

**This asymmetry is the problem.** As drafted, the agreement appears to require unanimous consent to give the founder's father shares, but not to give the founder himself more shares.

### Route 2 — Exhibit 2 is part of the Operating Agreement, so changing it is an amendment (strong)

The execution page states: *"the agreement constitutes, **together with Exhibit 1, Exhibit 2 and Exhibit 3 (if any), the Operating Agreement** of Bravado Spice LLC, adopted by the members as of Jun, 1st 2023."*

**Exhibit 2 is the member and share schedule.** Issuing new shares to anyone necessarily changes Exhibit 2 — and changing Exhibit 2 amends the Operating Agreement.

**The Operating Agreement contains no amendment provision at all.** The word "amend" appears only in incidental references ("Exhibit 2 as amended from time to time"), never as a procedure. Where a Texas company agreement is silent on how it may be amended, the statutory default governs (TBOC Chapter 101) — **counsel should confirm, but the default rule requires the consent of each member.** If so, **any issuance of new membership interests, including to the founder, requires unanimous written member consent.**

Assert both routes. Route 2 is the one that closes the gap.

### A related trap worth noting

Article 3.1 allocates profits and losses "in proportion to each Member's relative capital interest… **as set forth in Exhibit 2 as amended from time to time**," and Article 4.1 elects Managers by "a vote of the Members holding a majority of the capital interests… as set forth in Exhibit 2 as amended from time to time." Neither says who may amend Exhibit 2 or how. Whoever controls that document controls both economics and voting. Fixing this is the single most valuable amendment available.

## Part 4 — Proposing the amendment

### The practical problem

If amendments require unanimous consent, **Jeremiah must sign** any amendment restricting his own issuances. He will not do that for nothing.

### The leverage

He currently needs things that only the members can give:

1. **Deferral of the Nguyen loan and Blake's loan to 2027** — both marked in his own debt schedule as *"Requires your approval."*
2. **Whatever consent the entity-classification problem requires** — per Mark Clark's August 25 text, the S election is invalid while Comatri holds an interest, and no 2025 K-1s can issue until it is resolved. Any fix runs through Blake.
3. **Any conversion of his father's line of credit to equity** — §1.9 unanimous consent, which Blake and Hugh each hold individually.

**The amendment should be presented as the condition of that package**, not as a standalone demand: the members will consider the deferrals and cooperate on the tax fix, upon adoption of a short written amendment.

### Draft amendment language (for counsel to revise)

> **AMENDMENT NO. 1 TO THE COMPANY OPERATING AGREEMENT OF BRAVADO SPICE LLC**
>
> The undersigned, constituting all of the Members of Bravado Spice LLC (the "Company"), hereby amend the Company Operating Agreement adopted June 1, 2023 (the "Agreement") as follows:
>
> **1. New Article 1.10 — Issuance of Additional Interests.** No membership interest, share, unit, or other equity interest in the Company shall be issued, and Exhibit 2 shall not be amended, without the prior unanimous written consent of the Members. This restriction applies to any issuance to an existing Member as well as to any new Member, and applies whether the consideration is cash, property, services, the conversion or cancellation of indebtedness, accrued or deferred compensation, or any other consideration.
>
> **2. New Article 5.3 — Manager Compensation.** Notwithstanding Article 5.1, the compensation of any Manager, including salary, deferred or accrued compensation, bonus and any accrual recorded as a liability of the Company, shall be established in advance in writing and approved by Members holding a majority of the capital interests, excluding the interests held by the Manager whose compensation is being determined. No amount shall be accrued or recorded as owing to a Manager for services except as so approved. Any accrual recorded prior to the date of this Amendment is subject to ratification by the same vote.
>
> **3. New Article 5.4 — Related-Party Transactions.** Any loan, guarantee, or other financial transaction between the Company and a Manager, a Member, or a person related to either shall be disclosed in writing to all Members within thirty (30) days, together with its material terms.
>
> **4. New Article 6.4 — Reporting.** Within thirty (30) days after the end of each calendar quarter, the Managers shall deliver to each Member a balance sheet, an income statement, and a schedule of all amounts paid or accrued to or on behalf of any Manager, Member, or related party during the period.
>
> **5. New Article 8.1 — Amendment.** This Agreement, including its Exhibits, may be amended only by an instrument in writing signed by all Members.
>
> All other provisions of the Agreement remain in full force and effect.
