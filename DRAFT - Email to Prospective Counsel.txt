NOTES BEFORE YOU SEND - not part of the email
=====================================================================

1. Privilege. Nothing you have written so far is privileged. Do not attach the full analysis to a cold email. Offer it, and send it once someone is engaged or has at least agreed to review under an engagement letter. The summary above is deliberately light on conclusions for that reason.

2. Tone. The email deliberately avoids characterizing anyone's conduct. Lawyers discount clients who arrive with conclusions and engage with clients who arrive with documents. Your organization is the strongest signal in this email, which is why it gets its own paragraph.

3. Practice areas. You likely want a closely-held business and LLC disputes litigator in Texas as the lead, plus a tax attorney or CPA for the S-election question. Ask each firm which of the two they are. A firm that claims both without qualification is worth a follow-up question.

4. Proportionality is stated on purpose. Naming the $58,000 figure up front filters out firms that would take the matter and bill past its value, and it signals that you are a realistic client. If the Nguyens join, the combined exposure is closer to $258,000, which changes the calculus. That is why the sentence about their participation is there.

5. Do not send this to Mark Clark's or the company's counsel, and do not discuss it with Mark. He is a member, a creditor of the company, and a potential witness.

6. Ask each firm about: conflicts; whether they would represent Comatri alone or Comatri and the Nguyens jointly; a flat fee for the records demand phase alone; whether they would take the tax question in-house or refer it; and whether they would consider a contingency or hybrid arrangement on any fiduciary-duty or oppression claim.
