# Counsel Shortlist — Ranked, with Reasoning

**Prepared:** August 26, 2026. Compiled by web search from a sandboxed environment; several firm websites were unreachable. **I could not verify firm size, attorney counts, board certifications, current rosters, or quality of representation for any firm below.** This is a call order and a set of decision criteria, not an endorsement. Verify board certification independently at tbls.org and run your own conflicts check.

## The finding that should drive your sequencing

**Texas provides fee-shifting on books-and-records enforcement.** If you make a proper written demand stating a proper purpose, the company refuses, and a court orders production, **the company pays your attorney's fees and costs.** (Tex. Bus. Orgs. Code ch. 101, subch. K; the same principle appears in the corporate provisions.)

That changes the economics of phase one completely:

- The records demand is the **cheapest, lowest-risk, highest-information** step available, and it is partly self-funding if he refuses.
- It is a **discrete, well-defined task** — exactly the kind of work to buy on a flat fee.
- You do **not** need to decide about the larger claims before taking it. Get the records, then decide.

So hire for the records demand first, on a fixed price, and treat the fiduciary-duty and valuation questions as a separate later decision with a possibly different firm.

**A second point worth knowing:** the S-election defect is very likely **fixable**. The IRS has established relief for late and defective S elections and for inadvertent terminations. This matters for tone — you are not holding a doomsday device, you are holding a problem that requires everyone's cooperation to fix, and your signature is part of the fix. That is a more durable form of leverage than a threat.

## Ranking criteria used

1. Demonstrated focus on **LLC** member disputes specifically — not just corporate shareholder oppression, which is a different statute and a different body of law
2. Evidence of actual **books-and-records** practice, not a generic practice-area page
3. **Economics fit** — a $58K matter (or $258K with the Nguyens) should be normal-sized for them
4. **Partner-level attention** likely
5. **Harris County** — the company is in Houston, the Operating Agreement chooses Texas law, and any suit lands there
6. Fee flexibility: flat fee for phase one, hybrid or contingency available later

## Tier 1 — call these first

### 1. Hendershot Cowart P.C. (Houston, founded 1987)

**Why first:** the strongest evidence of focus on your exact problem. They maintain a dedicated LLC member-disputes practice page, a partnership/shareholder dispute page, a breach-of-fiduciary-duty page, **and multiple substantive articles specifically about shareholder and member rights to inspect books and records** (published November 2022 and again April 2026). Writing repeatedly about the narrow procedure you need is better evidence of real practice than a services list. They describe themselves as offering boutique-level attention with larger-firm capability, which is the size band you want.

**Verify:** attorney count, whether a board-certified attorney would handle it, and whether they will quote a flat fee for the demand.

### 2. Showalter Colgin & Davis, PLLC (Houston, since 1977)

**Why:** their stated scope is unusually precise for this matter — partnership dissolutions, shareholder oppression, breach of fiduciary duty, buy-sell enforcement, business-valuation disputes, minority squeeze-outs, and specifically **"violations of operating agreements."** That last phrase is LLC language rather than corporate language, which is the distinction most firms blur. Long-established and small.

**Verify:** current size and whether they are taking new matters of this scale.

## Tier 2 — strong candidates, especially for a later phase

### 3. Sears Crawford LLP (Houston)

**Why:** takes commercial litigation on **contingency**, which directly addresses your economics problem. Be realistic about what that means: contingency firms want damages cases, and a records demand is not one. This is a phase-two call — worth having early anyway, because a contingency firm's willingness to look at your fiduciary-duty and draw claims is the most honest read you will get on whether those claims are actually worth money.

### 4. Crain Caton & James (Houston)

**Why:** established Houston firm with a fiduciary-litigation practice that explicitly offers **either hourly or contingency**, tailored per matter. Useful precisely because they will discuss both structures.

### 5. Mahendru, P.C. / The Litigation Group (Houston)

**Why:** described as having built a reputation representing shareholders, partners and equity holders in disputes over **closely-held Houston companies** — the right client profile. Boutique.

## Tier 3 — for one specific question

### 6. Chamberlain Hrdlicka (Houston) — the tax question only

**Why:** this is the most specific match found for your actual entity problem. The firm's tax controversy group includes an attorney whose stated experience is working with the **IRS Business Entity Department to revive late and defective S corporation elections.** That is precisely the issue Mark Clark described.

They are a large, nationally-known tax firm, so do not hire them to run a member dispute — but a scoped opinion on "what election exists, is it valid, what relief is available, and whose consent does the fix require" is exactly what they do. Your CPA may be able to answer it more cheaply; ask them first, and escalate here if the answer is uncertain.

### 7. Roberts Markland LLP; Johnson Garcia LLP (both Houston)

Plausible but with less evidence of closely-held/LLC specialization in what I could see. Backups.

## Not for this matter

- **Burk Law Firm** (Austin) — well-regarded business divorce practice since 1992, but wrong county. Their published writing on shareholder oppression and business divorce is worth reading regardless.
- **Wade McClure / Mayer LLP** and **Vestige Law** — Dallas-oriented.
- **Winstead** and **Bradley** — both publish excellent business-divorce material (Winstead's "Business Divorce: Minority Shareholder Rights In Texas" is a genuinely useful free primer, as is Bradley's business divorce blog). **Read them; don't hire them.** Big-firm rates against a $58K exposure make no sense.

## What to ask on every call

1. Have you brought a TBOC §101.502 records demand for an **LLC** member, and have you had to sue to enforce one?
2. Will you quote a **flat fee** for phase one — review my draft demand, send it, evaluate the response? *(If they won't fix a price on a defined task, that tells you something.)*
3. Do you pursue the **fee-shifting** provision when the company refuses?
4. Who actually does the work, and are they board certified in Civil Trial Law?
5. Would you consider a **contingency or hybrid** on the fiduciary-duty and draw claims? *(The real test of whether they think the claims have value.)*
6. Do you handle the S-election question in-house or refer it?
7. Could you represent Comatri and the Nguyens jointly, and how would you handle the conflicts waiver?
8. Conflicts: Bravado Spice LLC, Jeremiah Tallerine, Mark Clark, Hugh and Christy Nguyen, William S. Annon CPA, Strata Cloud Accountants.

## How to re-rank after the calls

My ranking is based on published materials only. **Yours should be based on one thing: which lawyer, on the phone, immediately understood the difference between the LLC statute and the corporate statute, and told you something about your situation you did not already know.** That is the whole test. Anyone who has to look up §101.502 while you are talking goes to the bottom of the list regardless of where I placed them.
