Six questions for the meeting, in order

  1. Cash. How much is in the bank today, what is past due, and what must be paid in the next 13 weeks? Ask for the payables list by vendor, including how much is owed to Endorphin Farms and to Mark.
  2. Who runs the company. How many hours a week does Jer work on Bravado, and for how many more months does he intend to run it? The July document says he took outside jobs. The books accrued salary for him at $75,000 a year in 2023, 2024 and 2025. Ask who would take over the bank accounts, customers and books.
  3. What Jer owes the company. Which of the four figures in chart 2 is right, and does Jer agree he owes it? Is there a note, interest, or a repayment date? Will it be repaid in cash or set off against accrued wages? Why are you asked to defer your loan while it is outstanding?
  4. What the company pays for Jer. Payroll ended in March. About $49,200 went to him or to card accounts in his name from January 1 to July 22. Mark's draft expenses $31,312 as "Unallocated reimbursements." Where are the expense reports, which card accounts were paid, and who decides whether a payment to him is a distribution, a reimbursement, or a loan?
  5. The books. Jer has said they are being redone. Is Mark's September column the redone version, and which ledger is it from? Who gave Finaloop its opening balances? Is the $69,033 owed to Jer or by Jer? Ask that the QuickBooks file and the Finaloop ledger be kept unchanged while anything is redone.
  6. The tax status. Who chose January 1, 2025 as the date Comatri acquired its shares, and what did the CPA have in front of him? Who are the six shareholders on the 2024 return, and can the members see all six 2024 K-1s? Has anyone advised whether 2024 must be refiled?

If time allows

  • Your note. How many payments have been missed, and is interest accruing?
  • The Tallerine note. Described as "Forgiven" in July; carried at $79,440 on Mark's draft after $10,000 more was borrowed on July 28. Is there anything in writing from the lender?
  • Ownership. The 2025 return puts Jer at 7,000 of 10,700 shares. The named holders total 10,500. Who holds the other 200?
  • Debt written off to income. $51,405 in 2025 and $23,217 in 2024. Whose cards were they, and who is paying them now?
  • The turnaround plan. A monthly sales number, a margin number, and three priorities with dates.
