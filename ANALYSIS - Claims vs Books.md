# Bravado Spice — Investor Claims vs. the Books

**Prepared:** August 24, 2026
**Scope:** Cross-check of Jeremiah Tallerine's investor communications (Oct 2024 pitch materials, Jul 22 2026 "State of the Business," Aug 24 2026 "State of Bravado" briefing, and the daily cash plans) against the company's own records in this repository: QuickBooks P&L 2023–2026, QuickBooks Balance Sheet (run Aug 24 2026), QuickBooks General Ledger (Jan 2020–Aug 25 2026), QuickBooks audit logs 2020–2026, and the Finaloop general ledger export (2026 YTD, exported Jul 23 2026).

Every number below is traceable to a file in this repo. "The books" means QuickBooks unless noted; Finaloop is the 2026 system of record (QuickBooks activity effectively stops in Q1 2026).

## 1. Executive summary

The core *trajectory* story told to investors — margins really did improve dramatically, overhead really was cut, the 2024/2023 collapse was real — is supported by the books. The Aug 24 plan-vs-actual table honestly quotes the locked July plan. But the presentation systematically **removes Jeremiah's own compensation and cash extraction from the loss numbers**, and the "founder sacrifice" narrative ("skipped my own paychecks," "took outside jobs to put more money into Bravado," "all personal capital is in this business") is **contradicted by the company's own ledgers**, which show:

- **~$93K net flowed to Jer or his personal creditors during 2025** (bi-monthly $2,500 draws plus company payments of his personal Apple Card, Citi, Capital One, Credit One, and Bank of America cards — GL account *33500 Due to Jeremiah*, net change −$88,082, plus *Jer's Draw* −$5,331);
- **~$50K more flowed to him in Jan–Jul 2026** — the same period he described to investors as a near-death cash crisis ($21,752 booked as *Distributions — Jeremiah Tallerine*, $27,640 booked as *Reimbursements to owners* paying his personal Capital One/Petal cards, ~$0.9K of identified personal spending from company accounts — Finaloop GL), against only $3,830 he put back in;
- On the company's own books, after crediting **all** of his accrued unpaid wages ($176,914), Jer's net position is roughly **zero (QuickBooks, Dec 2025: company owed him only ~$8K net)** to **negative ~$50K (Finaloop, Jul 2026: he owes the company)**. The unpaid-paycheck sacrifice was, in cash terms, already recovered.
- The **2025 loss shown to investors was reduced by exactly $69,033** — the year-end journal entry accruing his own unpaid wages (**AJE-547, 12/31/2025**) — while the 2024 comparison year *includes* his wage accruals, overstating the year-over-year improvement.
- The **H1 2026 operating loss shown to investors (−$21,974) is about 2.6× smaller than the ledger's (−$57,295)**; the gap is almost exactly the $27,520 of "Reimbursements to owners" (money to Jer) plus $17,587 of bad debt excluded from his presentation.

## 3. Clear misrepresentations (claim vs. ledger, with citations)

### 3.1 The 2025 loss shown to investors excludes Jer's own accrued wages — exactly $69,033

Jul 22 doc claims (labeled "**(books)**" / "Finaloop accrual books"): 2025 OpEx **$353,657**, operating loss **−$202,799**, net loss **−$46,157**.

QuickBooks 2025 P&L: OpEx **$422,690**, operating loss **−$271,832**, net loss **−$115,190**.

Every one of the three differences is **$69,033.00** — the exact amount of QuickBooks journal entry **AJE-547, dated 12/31/2025, "Accrue Unpaid Wages Due to Jeremiah"** (GL, account *Wages Due to Jeremiah*, balance $107,881 → $176,914).

- The 2024 comparison figures in the same document **include** his wage accruals (2024 matches QB to the dollar, and *Wages Due to Jeremiah* grew +$39,131 during 2024). Removing his accrual only from 2025 makes the improvement look far better than like-for-like: OpEx "cut $591K → $354K" (−40%) vs. actual $591K → $423K (−28.5%); "underlying operating loss cut from $360K to $203K" vs. actual $360K → $272K.
- The revenue, COGS, gross-profit, and other-income figures in the same table all match QuickBooks exactly — the **only** altered line is the one containing his own pay.

### 3.2 The H1 2026 operating loss shown to investors is ~2.6× understated, and the excluded items are payments to Jer

Jul 22 doc: 2026 YTD (Jan–Jun) operating loss **−$21,974**.

Finaloop ledger (his own 2026 system of record, exported Jul 23 2026 — the file in this repo): net revenue $164,673, product COGS $43,563, fulfillment/selling costs $25,165, operating expenses $153,241 → **operating loss −$57,295**.

The −$21,974 figure is reachable only by excluding **"Reimbursements to owners" $27,520** (transfers to Jer's personal Capital One accounts and payments of his personal Petal/Capital One cards) and **bad debt $17,587** from expenses (−57,295 + 27,520 + 17,587 ≈ −12,188; with his slightly different revenue cut of $141,119 the arithmetic lands at ≈ −$22K). Both are real operating expenses in the ledger; one of them is money that went to him.

### 3.3 The founder-sacrifice narrative is contradicted by the ledgers

Claims (Jul 22 doc): *"Took multiple outside jobs to put more money into Bravado and to make skipping my own paychecks possible."* *"All personal credit and personal capital is in this business."*

The books:

- **2025** — GL account *33500 Due to Jeremiah* went from ~$0 to **−$88,082** (a debit balance: payments to him beyond anything owed in that account). The transaction stream is bi-monthly **$2,500 transfers to his personal Capital One 5820 / CHK 0002** (one memo literally reads "OWNERS BI-MONTHLY DRAW," 11/13/2025) plus company payments of his **personal Apple Card ($10,890.90 on 1/30/2025), Citi, Credit One, Bank of America, and Chase personal cards**. This balance is already *net* of ~$20K of credits for documented business expenses he fronted (AJE-406, 435/436, 447–449, 458, 465/466, 474, 481/482, 490, 504–507, 540, 545/546). Plus *Jer's Draw* −$5,331. **Net ~$93K to Jer or his personal creditors in 2025.**
- **Jan–Jul 2026** — Finaloop: *Distributions — Jeremiah Tallerine* **$21,752** (e.g., $2,500 on Jan 14, Jan 30, Feb 18, Feb 27…), *Reimbursements to owners* **$27,640** (largely his personal Capital One and Petal card payments; booked as expense with no expense detail), and ~**$0.9K** of identified personal spending from company accounts booked to his owner receivable — **during the exact months he described as the $107 cash crisis**. Those personal items paid from the company's Found account and company card in May–July 2026 include Talkiatry (therapy), Roots Art Therapy (Brooklyn), Nitehawk Cinema, GoPuff, a pharmacy, and a OneMain personal-loan payment. He contributed back $3,830 (*Capital — Jeremiah Tallerine*). **Net ~$46–50K out to Jer in H1 2026.**
- **Net position**: crediting him for *all* accrued unpaid wages ($176,914 — the "skipped paychecks"), QuickBooks at 12/31/2025 shows the company owed him only **~$8K net** ($176,914 wages − $88,082 overdrawn account − $75,441 *Due from Jer* − $5,331 draws). Finaloop's opening balances go further: an owner receivable of **$226,744 due FROM Jer** as of Jul 2026 vs. the $176,914 payable — i.e., on the 2026 books **he owes the company ~$50K net**.
- The timing matters: he moved **~$33K to personal accounts in January–February 2026 alone** (the strong post-holiday collection months), and the company then hit its cash floor in April–July — the crisis attributed in the docs entirely to macro conditions and Buc-ee's.

"Skipped paychecks" is narrowly true (Gusto payroll to him stopped; wages accrued unpaid). But the overall picture presented — personal sacrifice, money flowing *in* — is the opposite of what the ledgers show for 2025–2026.

### 3.4 The $226,744 owner receivable is never disclosed

Both 2026 investor documents present detailed creditor tables, down to a $344 Uline balance. Neither mentions that the largest single receivable on the company's books is **$226,744 due from the founder** (Finaloop: *Due to/from owner — non-interest balance — Jeremiah Tallerine*; QuickBooks predecessors: *Due from Jer* $75,441 + overdrawn *Due to Jeremiah* $88,082 + draws). Investors deciding whether to defer their own loans (Nguyen $88,767; Blake Benthall personally $7,500 — a short-term bridge loan agreed to be repaid as **$8,500 within one month**, per Blake) were never told the founder's balance exists.

### 3.5 "July 7, 2026: the operating account held $107"

The Found-account ledger in Jer's own Finaloop export shows an end-of-day balance of **~$2,304 on July 7**, and a period low of **~$1,713 (July 3)**. The $107 figure appears in the cash-plan workbook as a **hand-typed input** ("Starting Bank (Jul 7): 107 ← edit to match actual Found balance"). Unless it was a momentary intraday dip not visible at day granularity, the dramatic "$107" anchor of the recovery narrative is not supported by the ledger. (Chase accounts were ~$0/negative and PayPal negative at the time, so no other account explains it.)

### 3.6 "Tallerine line of credit $69,440 — Forgiven"

The Jul 22 doc lists his father's LOC as forgiven, and the "$736K total liabilities at year-end 2025" figure equals QuickBooks total liabilities ($805,512) **minus** this loan ($69,440) — exactly. But the loan is still carried as a liability in **both** QuickBooks (through the Aug 24 2026 report run) and Finaloop (Jul 2026: *Line of credit — Anthony Tallerine* $69,440.20), and no forgiveness income was ever booked in either system. Either the books are wrong or the representation is; they cannot both be right. (Note: if it *had* been forgiven in 2025, GAAP would put ~$69K of forgiveness income through the P&L — which would coincidentally produce a net loss close to the −$46,157 shown. Instead the deck got there by deleting expenses. Same bottom line, very different operating story.)

### 3.7 Debt tables that don't sum, and omitted debts

- Aug 24 briefing: the itemized creditor rows (excluding, as stated, rent and the disputed Unishippers claim) sum to **~$598.3K**, not the stated **"$572,715"** — an unexplained ~$25.5K gap approximately equal to the SBG balance ($24,306). The Jul 22 "original total debt $563,065" similarly equals its own creditor rows **minus both SBG lines** ($16,614 + $13,312 = $29,926), despite SBG being listed as debt in the same document.
- **Shopify Capital** (~$8.5K in Finaloop, $13.1K at YE2025 in QB) never appears in either 2026 debt schedule.
- The **$176,914 accrued-wage liability** to Jer is never quantified anywhere (only the "skipped paychecks" narrative), and total balance-sheet liabilities per Finaloop at Jul 2026 are ≈ **$907K** vs. the ≈ $633K a reader can assemble from the Aug 24 briefing's disclosed pieces.

### 3.8 Framing: "2024 — the year the wheels came off"

The "three-year arc" starts at 2024 and attributes the collapse to the departed business partner's contracts. The books show **2023 was the worst year in company history — net loss $564,654** (QB 2023 P&L; equity already −$366K at Dec 2023), and Jer's own Oct 2024 pitch sheet showed 2023 cash-from-operations of −$582K and negative operating cash in 2022. The collapse predates the story's starting point; starting the arc at 2024 makes the narrative cleaner ("partner's fault, then my turnaround") than the record supports. (The *Due from James* $56,369 receivable does corroborate that the ex-partner owes the company money.)

## 7. Method notes

- QuickBooks 2026 is incomplete (books moved to Finaloop ~Q1 2026); all 2026 figures here use the Finaloop export (through Jul 22–23, 2026).
- Found daily balances were back-computed from the Finaloop Found register and its stated ending balance ($3,676.44 at export); the register's implied opening balance is exactly $0.00 at Jan 1, 2026, suggesting completeness.
- The H1 2026 operating-loss reconstruction groups Finaloop accounts as: net revenue = sales + shipping income − discounts/deductions − refunds + fulfillment reimbursements; COGS = product COGS; fulfillment/selling = pick-pack, FBA/Walmart fees, freight-out, marketplace fees, warehouse; OpEx = all remaining P&L expense accounts excluding interest/bank charges. Reasonable alternative groupings move the result by low single-digit thousands — nowhere near the $35K gap to the claimed figure.
