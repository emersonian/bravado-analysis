# Which Payments to Jer Were Legitimate? A Substantiation Analysis

**Prepared:** August 25, 2026
**Question:** Of the money that flowed from Bravado to Jeremiah or his personal creditors, which transfers were legitimate reimbursements of business expenses he fronted, and which were not? Blake's proposed test — *a payment is legitimate when it was matched by split/categorized expense entries* — turns out to be exactly the test Bravado's own bookkeepers applied, which lets us use their contemporaneous judgments instead of ours.

---

## 1. How the machinery actually worked (and why the books already contain the verdict)

The QuickBooks account **"33500 Due to Jeremiah"** operated as a clearing account with two sides:

- **Substantiation (credits):** when Jer submitted business expenses he'd paid personally, the bookkeepers posted an itemized journal entry — debiting the *real* expense categories ("-Split-") and crediting 33500, creating a genuine payable to him. Examples across the years: "Jer Reimbursement for 2023" (monthly, AJE-76…87), "Reimbursement of COGS material," "Reimbursement to Jer - The boxery," "- Roll labels," "- coverdash," "Reimbursements Jer - Spirit Airlines," "Reimbursement of Hertz, Shell Oil, Walmart."
- **Payments (debits):** cash out — transfers to his personal bank accounts, or the company paying his personal credit-card bills — drawing the payable down.

When payments ran **ahead** of substantiation, the account went into a debit balance: money out with nothing documented behind it. And here is the decisive point: **every year-end, the accountants swept that unsubstantiated excess into a receivable — "Due from Jer" — with memos in their own words:** "to equate member draws" (2020), "to reclass [from draw]" (2022), "to equate drawing accounts" (Jul 2023), and "**per CPA - consolidate the Due To/From accounts for Jeremiah**" (Dec 31, 2024). They did not book it as expense, and they did not charge it against his accrued wages. Their treatment *is* the classification: substantiated → expense; unsubstantiated → Jer owes it back.

One more piece of their judgment is on the record. In **January–February 2025**, payments of his personal cards (Apple Card $10,891, BofA, Citi, MasterCard) and transfers were initially charged against his accrued **wages** — a treatment that would have made them legitimate wage payments. The accountants then **reversed that**: AJE-387/388 ("To reclass January/February 2025 trx Wages") moved ~$21.4K of those payments *out* of wages and *into* the owner clearing account. They looked at these exact transactions and decided they were not payroll.

Three things jump out:

1. **The system worked for years.** 2022–2024 substantiation ($46K, $39K, $45K) tracked or exceeded the card-payment volume. Jer genuinely fronted large business costs — Erewhon slotting fees, freight, packaging, materials — and the books prove it. This is why a blanket "the reimbursements were fake" claim would be wrong and would fail.
2. **2025 is where legitimacy collapsed.** Cash out quintupled relative to substantiation. The bookkeepers kept soliciting documentation all year (23 itemized entries — they booked everything they were given); the money simply outran it 5:1.
3. **2026 has no substantiation at all.** The $27,640 of "Reimbursements to owners" are lump card-bill payments booked to a single expense line, no splits, no receipts in the system — the process didn't degrade, it stopped.

## 3. A three-tier answer to "which were legitimate?"

**Tier A — Legitimate by the books' own test (~$153K, 2022–2025).** The itemized split-JE credits. Caveat in both directions: "substantiated in the books" means the bookkeepers received line-item detail, not that receipts were audited — and two 2025 entries are chunky and generic ("Jer's reimbursement 05.08.2025" $6,412.70; "Hertz, Shell Oil, Walmart" $4,820.93 — rental cars and gas that postdate his NYC move, worth a policy question even if real).

**Tier B — Legitimate-ish: wage paydowns ($35,644, all in 2024).** These stayed booked as payments of genuinely accrued salary. They are not misappropriation — but they do mean Jer received ~$36K of back pay in 2024, which sits awkwardly beside "skipped my own payroll entirely for long runs." *(Correction to our earlier working figures: 2024 payments to Jer were larger than previously described, but $35.6K of them were properly wage-classified — the 2024 picture is materially cleaner than 2025's.)*

**Tier C — Indeterminate without his card statements (~$27K in 2025, ~$27.6K in 2026).** Payments of his personal credit-card *bills* beyond what was substantiated. A card bill hides its merchants: these could contain undocumented business charges, personal spending, or both. Nobody — including Bravado's own bookkeepers — can classify them from the company's books. Only his Capital One/Petal/Citi/Apple/BofA/Credit One statements and receipts can. (Under IRS accountable-plan rules, unsubstantiated reimbursements default to taxable compensation — the burden of proof runs toward him, not us.)

**Tier D — Presumptively personal, no reimbursement theory available (~$66K in 2025; ~$22.7K in 2026).**
- 2025: ~**$61K of fixed transfers to his personal bank accounts** (the bi-monthly $2,500s to Capital One Personal Checking 5820 / CHK 0002 — one memo'd "OWNERS BI-MONTHLY DRAW"), plus $5,331 booked to "Jer's Draw." Fixed, recurring, round-number transfers to personal checking are compensation-shaped, not expense-shaped; no one reimburses expenses at $2,500 twice a month for a year.
- 2026: **$21,752 booked by Finaloop as "Distributions – Jeremiah Tallerine"** (a distribution is by definition not a reimbursement) plus **$924 of merchant-identified personal spending** (therapy, cinema, GoPuff, his OneMain loan) that Finaloop charged to his receivable.

## 4. What this does to our headline numbers (self-audit)

- The **"$93K net out to Jer in 2025"** figure survives — it was already computed *net of* the $22,612 substantiated (that's why it equals the $88,082 balance + $5,331 draws). But it should be described as: **~$66K presumptively personal + ~$27K indeterminate pending his card statements**, not $93K of proven personal benefit.
- The **"~$50K to Jer in 2026"** figure should likewise be stated as a range: **$22.7K unambiguous + $27.6K unsubstantiated card-bill payments.**
- Combined 2025–Jul 2026: **floor ~$89K of clear personal benefit; ceiling ~$144K** depending on what his card statements show. Both briefs have been updated to this framing.
- **A reinterpretation that matters:** the "$75K I have drawn" in Jer's private draft is not a random number — it is the balance of "Due from Jer," i.e., **the accountants' own cumulative unsubstantiated-draw account through 2024** ($75,441). He used the correct concept and the correct account. What he left out was the identical, larger 2025 tranche of exactly the same thing ($88,082, sitting one account over, in the ledger his CFO reconciled) plus the $5,331 draws. This makes the omission harder to attribute to confusion: he demonstrably knew which account measures his unsubstantiated draws, and cited the stale one.

## 5. How to resolve Tier C definitively

The records that settle it, in priority order — all within the §4.6 books-and-records right and standard for an expense-substantiation review:

1. **Personal card statements** for every card the company paid (Capital One ×2, Petal, Citi, Apple Card, BofA, Credit One, personal Chase), 2024–2026 — matched line-by-line against the company's payment dates.
2. **Receipts / expense submissions** behind the Tier A entries and any claimed for Tier C (the Finaloop receipt inbox, if used).
3. The **Finaloop classification correspondence** — whoever told (or failed to tell) Finaloop what the 2026 card payments were.
4. His **2025–2026 personal tax treatment** of the unsubstantiated amounts (were they reported as income or distributions?).

**The fair framing for the meeting:** "About $153K of Jer's reimbursements over the years were properly documented and we don't dispute them — the system worked when he used it. Our issue is the ~$89–144K since January 2025 that has no documentation behind it, half of which is fixed transfers to his personal checking account, during the period he told us he was taking nothing."
