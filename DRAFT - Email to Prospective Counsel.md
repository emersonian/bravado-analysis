## Notes before you send

1. **Privilege.** Nothing you've written so far is privileged. Don't attach the full analysis to a cold email — offer it, and send it once someone is engaged or has at least agreed to review under an engagement letter. The summary above is deliberately light on conclusions for that reason.
2. **Tone.** The email deliberately avoids characterizing anyone's conduct. Lawyers discount clients who arrive with conclusions; they engage with clients who arrive with documents. Your organization is the strongest signal in this email — that's why it gets its own paragraph.
3. **Practice areas.** You likely want a **closely-held business / LLC disputes** litigator in Texas as the lead, plus a **tax** attorney or CPA for the S-election question. Ask each firm which of the two they are; a firm that claims both without qualification is worth a follow-up question.
4. **Proportionality is stated on purpose.** Naming the ~$58,000 figure up front filters out firms that would take the matter and bill past its value, and it signals you're a realistic client. If the Nguyens join, the combined exposure is closer to $258,000, which changes the calculus — hence the sentence about their participation.
5. **Don't send this to Mark Clark's or the company's counsel**, and don't discuss it with Mark. He is a member, a creditor of the company, and a potential witness.
6. **Ask each firm about:** conflicts, whether they'd represent Comatri alone or Comatri and the Nguyens jointly, estimated cost for the records demand phase alone, and whether they'd take the tax question in-house or refer it.
