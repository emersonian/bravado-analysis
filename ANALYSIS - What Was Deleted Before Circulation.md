# What Was Deleted Between Blake's Private Draft and the Circulated Investor Document

**Prepared:** August 25, 2026
**Method:** Programmatic text and table diff of the two Word files, line by line, both stored in this repository:

| | File | Words | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Draft** | `Bravado_State_of_the_Business_Jul2026 (8-page, private to Blake pre-meeting).docx` | 2,761 | Sent privately to Blake Benthall **before** the July 22 meeting |
| **v4** | `2026-07-22-Bravado-State-of-Business-v4.docx` | 1,540 | Circulated to **all** investors (Clark, Hugh Nguyen, Christy Nguyen, Blake Benthall) |

v4 is 44% shorter, so length alone explains many cuts. This memo separates the cuts that brevity explains from the ones it cannot — and the test that settles it is simple: **where v4 kept a table the same size but changed its contents, shortening was not the reason.**

---

## 1. The finding in one paragraph

The private draft contains four statements that are demonstrably false against Bravado's own books, plus the line items a reader would need to detect them. In the circulated version, **all four statements and all of the detecting line items are gone — deleted, not corrected.** Nothing was substituted that fixes them. At the same time, v4 deleted several *honest* disclosures that made the draft more candid, and added new certainty claims that the draft never made. Both documents are incomplete, in different directions; the draft is more revealing about the founder's own money, and v4 is more revealing about third-party creditors.

## 3. Category B — The evidence trail was removed with it

These deletions matter more than they look, because each one is a line a reader could have used to *catch* a problem.

1. **The appendix line "of which payroll + 1099" — $276,587 (2024) vs $163,682 (2025).** This single row is the clearest proof of the $69,033 exclusion: the 2024 figure matches QuickBooks to within 25 cents, the 2025 figure is short by exactly $69,033.12. **Deleted from v4.** Brevity cannot explain it: v4's appendix *added* two new rows ("Interest & fees," "Year-end cash") while deleting this one and "of which rent." The table was re-composed, not trimmed.
2. **The K-1 / tax-return basis note** — *"our K-1 filings are cash basis, so the 2024 tax return shows a smaller loss ($67,869)… the 2024 federal return… shows $862,582 gross receipts and a $67,869 ordinary loss."* **Deleted.** This is the only place in any investor document that confirms pass-through returns and K-1s exist — the fact that makes the S-election/Comatri question answerable.
3. **"$177K of my unpaid wages sit on the balance sheet"** and the **$176,914 Deferred wages** table row. **Both deleted.** In v4 the sacrifice is narrated ("skipping my own paychecks") but never quantified anywhere — so a v4-only reader cannot compute the netting at all, and never learns the liability exists.
4. **Accounts payable $150,009** — *"After removing $20,231 of old bills already paid but still on the books (correction filed with Finaloop)"*. **Deleted.** (It ties exactly: QuickBooks A/P $170,240 − $20,231 = $150,009.)
5. **Shopify Capital $8,626**, *"16% of Shopify sales… due in full by June 2027."* **Deleted** — and also absent from the August briefing's debt schedule. Disclosed once, privately, then never again.

- **The qualifier on the $107:** *"It was the low-water mark of a cash cycle, not a default; **we have stayed current on the commitments that matter**."* Softened; v4 keeps "low-water mark of a cash cycle, not a default" but drops the rest.

- **The margin-basis caveat:** *"The 2024-2025 figures use the year-end management report's heavier COGS basis, so **compare the trend within each basis, not across them**."* **Deleted** — leaving v4's 28.7% → 32.4% → 59.4% arc presented as a clean like-for-like progression it is not.

Difference: −$220,323.** Both documents assert the same headline: *"Total liabilities were $736K at year-end 2025."* Neither itemization reconciles to it, and they cannot both be right. The removals are concentrated in **founder- and operations-related** liabilities; the additions are **third-party creditor** liabilities. A reader of v4 alone cannot see the founder's accrued wages, the trade payables, or the Shopify advance. A reader of the draft alone cannot see Endorphin Farms, the rent arrears, or Blake's own loan.

## 7. Assessment

**What the diff supports.** Every statement in the private draft that is now provably false was removed before the document went to the other investors, along with the specific line items that would have exposed it — while the same tables kept their size and gained new rows. That is not consistent with condensing for length; it is consistent with review. Someone read the draft between the private send and the circulation, and the edits landed on the sensitive material with precision.

**What the diff does not support.** It does not prove Jer knew the statements were false when he wrote them, and it does not show a single-minded effort to hide: v4 disclosed an $89,298 creditor the draft omitted, and dropped honest self-criticism ("the old $65K/month target was made up") that no one would cut to conceal anything. A plausible innocent account exists for parts of it — a CFO or adviser saying "don't put your unpaid-wage arithmetic in an investor document," "take the tax-return figures out," "tighten it." That account is worth testing rather than assuming away, because if an adviser directed the cuts, that adviser is a witness.

**What it changes.** Blake holds the only known copy of a version containing the founder's own draw arithmetic, the payroll-and-1099 appendix line, the tax-return figures, and the Shopify Capital debt. Those four items are the ones that convert "the presentation was optimistic" into "the presentation was checkable, and it fails." Preserve the draft file, its email, and its metadata.
