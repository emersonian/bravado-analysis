## 1. The 2025 loss in the circulated document excludes exactly one expense: Jer's own pay

**v4 told us** (three-year arc table and appendix, labeled "2025 **(books)**" / "from the Finaloop accrual books"):

> Operating expenses **$353,657** · Operating income **($202,799)** · Net income **($46,157)** · "Cut overhead nearly in half: … total OpEx $591K → $354K" · "Underlying operating loss cut from $360K to $203K"

**The books say:** OpEx **$422,690** · operating loss **$271,832** · net loss **$115,190**. Every line differs by **$69,033.00 — to the dollar, journal entry AJE-547, the accrual of Jer's own unpaid wages.** The 2024 comparison column in the same table matches QuickBooks to within 25 cents — *including* that year's founder-wage accrual. So the two years were prepared on different bases, and the only difference is his pay. The overhead cut was really $591K → $423K (−28%, not "nearly in half"), and the loss was cut to $272K, not $203K.

**He knew.** AJE-547 was created by his accountants Feb 26, 2026 (with a support PDF titled "Jer Wages Accrual - $69033.pdf"), edited three times by CFO Mark Clark in March–April, and Jer was logged into QuickBooks on July 19 — three days before the meeting. His probable defense — "the numbers came from Finaloop's December 2025 report" — fails because 2024 in the same table *is* the QuickBooks number, and because Finaloop's own balance sheet carries the full $176,914 wage liability.
