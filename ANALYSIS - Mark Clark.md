This memo assembles everything the records show about Clark's roles, what he knew and when, and his financial entanglements — then gives a calibrated verdict. Sources: QB audit logs, QB general ledger, Finaloop opening balances, Clark's own workbook files (author metadata "mark clark"), and the investor documents.

## 2. What Clark knew, and when (the early-knowledge record)

1. **He watched everything.** His QB usage is almost entirely *reading*: 29 sessions in May 2024, 12 in **October 2024 (the Comatri pitch month)**, 39 across Jan–Apr 2025. In four years his only substantive ledger actions are the three AJE-547 edits (§3).
2. **He authored the pitch model.** The budget workbooks carry his authorship metadata; the Oct 2024 package sent to Blake (2024/2025 budgets, income trend, the presentation that nets the one-time **AJE-229 $23,217 "per Jer this is not payable" write-off into June operating results**) is his work product. The historical columns in it are accurate to the dollar — the presentation choices are where the trouble lives.
3. **He built a ledger of Jer's draws.** His workbook contains a **"Jer Accts" tab** — a transaction-level reconstruction of "Due From Jeremiah" and "Due to Jeremiah," tracing company payments of Jer's personal Citi/Capital One/Chase cards back to 2022. The tab does not exist in the Oct 2024 version of the file and does exist in the Jan 2025 version — Clark built it in roughly **Dec 2024–Jan 2025**.
4. **He flagged the problem in writing — the same hour he saved that file.** The workbook's last-modified stamp is **Jan 16, 2025, 3:41 PM**; at 3:47 PM Clark emailed Jer: *"let's not make any discretionary cash payments until sales get back in line."* The CFO had just itemized the founder's draw accounts and told him to stop discretionary outflows. **The draws continued all year (~$93K net in 2025).**
5. **He edited the wage-accrual entry** (§3) in the six weeks before investor documents were issued that exclude its effect — documents addressed to him — whose "$75K I have drawn" claim is contradicted by his own Jer Accts tab.

## 3. The AJE-547 edits — what they were, and whether they matter

*(Correction to a common slip: the edits were Mark Clark's, not Hugh's. Hugh has never had QuickBooks access.)*

**What the record shows:**

| Date | Actor | Event |
|---|---|---|
| Feb 26, 2026, 8:20 am | Strata Cloud Accountants | **Added** AJE-547 ("Accrue Unpaid Wages Due to Jeremiah," $69,033, dated 12/31/2025); a support PDF "Jer Wages Accrual - $69033.pdf" was attached per the earlier audit-log extract |
| Feb 26, 9:03 am | Strata | Edited (43 minutes later — normal same-morning correction) |
| **Mar 31, 2026, 2:12 pm** | **Mark Clark** | Edited — on **Q1 close day**, consistent with a quarter-end review pass |
| **Apr 8, 2026, 12:16 and 12:19 pm** | **Mark Clark** | Edited twice, three minutes apart — consistent with save-then-fix (a memo, date, class, or attachment tweak) |

**What the edits changed: unknowable from these exports.** The QBO audit-log CSV records *that* an entry was edited, not the field-level diff. What we can prove is that the **amount never changed**: the wages-due balance is $176,913.70 in the March 2026 QB balance sheet, in the Aug 24 2026 QB report run, and in Finaloop's opening balances — all consistent with $69,033 intact throughout. So the edits were almost certainly memo/date/classification/attachment-level, or open-and-save during review. (QBO retains field-level audit history internally — a records demand can produce the actual diffs, and should.)

**Why they matter anyway — and why "multiple edits" is the wrong thing to focus on.** The repeated edits are probably mundane. What is *not* mundane is what they prove about knowledge: **the CFO had this exact entry open on three occasions in the six weeks before the July documents were issued** — documents sent to him as an investor — whose 2025 figures are QuickBooks *minus exactly this entry*, and whose founder-draw claim contradicts the draw ledger he personally maintains. Clark is the one person besides Jer who demonstrably possessed both facts the July document misstated. Either he reviewed those documents and said nothing, or he was never shown them before they went out — and either answer is important. There is no evidence he wrote the false sentences, and the "$101K net" line attributes its treatment to "CPA advising" (Annon), not Clark.

## 5. Verdict — is Mark clean?

**Not implicated as an author of the misstatements. Not clean-and-clear either.** The calibrated statement:

- **No evidence** Clark drafted the July documents, invented the $75K figure, or manipulated any ledger entry. His own books work is the *honest* layer — the QuickBooks record that exposes everything is partly his stewardship, his pitch historicals were exact, and his Jan 2025 email urged exactly the discipline that was ignored.
- **But he is structurally compromised**: $82.5K in fees, an undisclosed $25K IOU, equity from a debt conversion, his own loans repaid ahead of trade creditors, and continuing economic dependence on Jer's goodwill and the investors' patience.
- **And he is the knowledge nexus**: the person who introduced Hugh, built the draw ledger, flagged discretionary payments, edited the wage accrual, and then — as far as any record shows — stayed silent while documents contradicting his own workpapers went to the investors he brought in.
- The fair characterization for Hugh: *"I'm not accusing Mark of anything. But the person who brought you in has five conflicting roles, an undisclosed $25,000 claim on the company, and provably knew both facts the July document got wrong. He is a witness and a conflicted party — he cannot be our referee."*
