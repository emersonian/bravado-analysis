Points to decide
1. Rate. I used 10 percent a year (Finance Code 302.001(b)) because no written agreement sets a higher lawful rate. Is a lower figure, or "the maximum lawful rate," safer?
2. Recipients. The letter goes to the manager and the company because the borrower is not settled. Is that right, and should it avoid saying which one owes it?
3. Delivery. Section 305.103(c) lists delivery "personally, by telecopier, or by United States mail to the address shown on the most recent documents in the transaction." No document in the transaction shows an address. Which addresses, and is hand delivery plus certified mail enough?
4. Completeness. I understand a 2026 decision (Vu v. Medina) rejected a correction that left one charge unaddressed, so paragraph 3 cancels everything above principal and stated interest. Is anything missing?
5. Stating the discovery date. Does it help or hurt to state November 19 in the letter?
6. USDC. Should the letter say "7,500 USDC (US$7,500)" or stay with dollars for the amount owed?
7. A signed note. Should this be paired with a short promissory note naming the borrower, or would asking for one reopen the terms?
8. Earlier statements. A published page, an email to a co-investor and two August drafts state the advance with the $8,500 figure. Does this letter need to address them, or is taking them out of circulation enough?
9. Order. This should go out before the records demand or anything else that mentions the advance.
